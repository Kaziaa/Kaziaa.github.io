Trained as an experimentalist, I am driven by a curiosity to understand how nature reveals itself through biological systems and interactions. Alongside experimental work, I engage with chemoinformatics and computational tools—many created and freely shared by dedicated scientists—to explore biological complexity from a data-driven perspective. My research life sits at the intersection of molecular biology, structural biology, computation, and quantitative thinking in biology and medicine.

I hold a PhD in Molecular and Structural Biology from UiT – The Arctic University of Norway, and I have worked across both experimental and computational domains. My scientific training includes hands-on experience in cell biology, protein chemistry, immunology, and drug discovery. Over time, my work has increasingly focused on integrating various methods—such as protein crystallography, chemoinformatics, virtual screening, molecular modeling, and data analysis—with experimental biology to address complex biological questions.

I have particular research interests in ion channels,protein kinase,  gene regulation, and translational biomedical research, including the application of structure-based drug design and machine learning to identify novel modulators of biological targets. I am also deeply interested in how quantitative thinking, computation, and automation can accelerate discovery in life sciences.

Beyond research, I enjoy learning and building across disciplines, including software tools for scientific workflows, data engineering, and reproducible research practices. This website serves as a space to share my scientific interests, projects, writings, and reflections on life beyond equations.
