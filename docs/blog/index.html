Fyn Kinase: A Central Regulator in Brain Function, Neurodegeneration, and Cancer

Apr 03, 2026

🔬 Introduction Fyn kinase is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase belonging to the Src family and plays a critical role in the central nervous system (CNS). It regulates brain development, synaptic signaling, and is strongly implicated in neurodegenerative diseases and cancer. 🧩 Role in Brain Development and Function 🧠 CNS Myelination Fyn is essential for myelination. Loss of Fyn leads to significant myelin deficits in the forebrain. 🔗 Oligodendrocyte Differentiation Fyn interacts with α-tubulin and Tau to stabilize microtubules, enabling oligodendrocytes to form myelin sheaths.

Electrophysiology (in vivo)

Feb 02, 2026

In vivo electrophysiology is the technique of recording electrical signals from living cells (usually neurons) inside a living animal, such as mouse, rat, or zebrafish. Neurons communicate via electrical signals: Action potentials (spikes) — fast, around 1 ms Local field potentials (LFP) — slower population signals Recording is done as voltage versus time and can be obtained from single neurons or groups of neurons.
